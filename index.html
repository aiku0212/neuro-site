    Blood BDNF is the measure most often used to sell these interventions. Exercise has the strongest evidence for raising it. Yoga and meditation trend upward in small or uncontrolled studies, fasting results conflict, and psychedelics leave it unchanged. This is a result about one blood marker, not about whether each intervention changes the brain.

    In the table, an RCT is a randomized controlled trial, and SMD (standardized mean difference) gives the size of an effect in standard deviations, where 0 means no effect. Exercise is the only one of these with pooled randomized evidence of a rise. Intensity seems to matter. In a 12-person crossover study, in which each person exercised at every intensity, high-intensity intervals raised serum BDNF more than moderate or low intensity, which did not differ from each other.

    BDNF signalling versus BDNF concentration

    BDNF signaling (what the protein does at its receptor) and BDNF concentration (how much of it is present) are different quantities, measured in different places. In cell and mouse studies, LSD and psilocin bound the TrkB receptor about a thousand times more tightly than the antidepressants tested and enhanced signalling from existing BDNF. In people, they do not raise the amount circulating in blood.

    A drug can change how a receptor works without changing how much BDNF is present.

    That may be why a meta-analysis of 29 studies found no change while cellular studies find large effects. Its authors raise a second explanation, that the animal findings may not fully carry over to humans. The current data cannot separate the two.

    How and why critical periods close

    Critical periods are times early in life when experience changes brain circuits most easily. They do not fade away. Molecular brakes actively shut them. These brakes include perineuronal nets, mesh-like coatings that wrap inhibitory interneurons and limit further rewiring of the circuit.

    That closure helps stabilize what the circuit has learned. Songbird species show how much this varies. Zebra finches close their learning period firmly, and their song becomes fixed, a step called crystallization. Starlings keep their learning period open and learn new songs every year. Both strategies have persisted through evolution, and neither is simply better.

    So the goal is not maximum plasticity. It is plasticity you can open and close on purpose.
