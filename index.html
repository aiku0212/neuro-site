Presynaptic terminal → cleft → dendritic spine

    Blood BDNF is the measure most often used to sell these interventions. Exercise has the strongest evidence for moving it. Yoga and meditation trend upward in small or uncontrolled studies, fasting results conflict, and psychedelics leave it unchanged. This is a result about one blood marker, not about whether each intervention changes the brain.

    Exercise is the only one of these with pooled randomized evidence of a rise. Intensity seems to matter: in a 12-person crossover study, high-intensity intervals raised serum BDNF more than moderate or low intensity, which did not differ from each other.

    The distinction almost everyone misses

    BDNF signaling and BDNF concentration are different quantities in different compartments. In cell and mouse studies, LSD and psilocin bound the TrkB receptor about a thousand times more tightly than the antidepressants tested and enhanced signalling from existing BDNF. In people, they do not raise the amount circulating in blood.

    A drug can transform how a receptor behaves without changing how much protein floats past it.

    That may be why a meta-analysis of 29 studies found no change while cellular studies find large effects. Its authors raise a second explanation: the animal findings may not fully carry over to humans. The current data cannot separate the two.

    Why closing matters

    Critical periods do not fade. They are actively shut by molecular brakes — perineuronal nets that wrap inhibitory interneurons and lock the circuit down.

    That closure helps stabilize what the circuit has learned. Songbirds show the range: zebra finches close hard and their song crystallizes; starlings stay open and learn new songs every year. Both strategies survived evolution. Neither is the upgrade.

    Which reframes the goal. Not maximum plasticity — plasticity you can open and close on purpose.
