Why the shape is the whole story

A spine is not decoration on a dendrite. It is a chemical compartment with a deliberately narrow neck, and that neck is a resistor. It isolates whatever happens in the head from the rest of the cell, so one synapse can change its own strength without dragging its neighbors with it. Widen the neck and the compartment leaks. Grow the head and the synapse gets stronger, because head volume tracks the number of AMPA receptors in the membrane above it almost linearly.

The four shapes are four life stages

Classifying spines by shape sounds like taxonomy for its own sake until you watch them over days. Thin spines appear and vanish within hours. Mushroom spines persist for months and carry the large postsynaptic densities. The shape is a readout of how long a synapse has survived and how much it currently matters, which is why a drug that shifts the population toward mushroom is interesting and a drug that merely adds filopodia is not.
