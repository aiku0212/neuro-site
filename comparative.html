Comparative Neuropsychiatry

The receptor is conserved.
The patient cannot answer.

Everything else on this site measures plasticity in one species — the one that can describe what a drug feels like. Dogs are prescribed the same molecules, acting on the same receptors, and cannot describe anything. That gap is not a limitation of veterinary medicine. It is a central open problem in animal neuroscience, and parts of it are measurable.

If your pet takes one of these medications

This page is research commentary, not veterinary advice. Adverse-event reports cannot show that a drug caused a problem, or how often it happens. Do not stop or change a pet's medication, or give a pet a medication prescribed for a person, without talking to a veterinarian. About and limits

Shared pharmacology

The Synapse Bench on the Evidence page models fluoxetine binding TrkB weakly, and ketamine disinhibiting the local network. Both of those drugs are given to dogs. So are most of the others.

Indications are approximate and partly off-label. Only fluoxetine's canine approval has been checked against a label so far. The point of the table is the overlap, not the detail.

The observability collapse

The percentages are measured; the reading below them is a hypothesis. openFDA, November 2026. Gabapentin reaction profiles, canine versus human, drawn from the two FDA spontaneous-reporting systems.

Human self-report → owner observation · gabapentin

Measured Percentages are openFDA report counts. The converging lines show the hypothesis, not data.

The naive read is that dogs are five times more prone to gabapentin lethargy. (The human figure pools two terms: 0.59% of reports say lethargy and 2.71% say somnolence, 3.21% with either. Treating them as one symptom is a judgment call, and using lethargy alone would make the gap far larger.) That read is probably wrong, and the reason is the whole problem. About a fifth of human gabapentin reports list an internal state — pain, fatigue, nausea, headache, dizziness or anxiety — that is hard to see from outside. Owners can record some of it: pain appears in 5.33% of dog reports. A dog experiencing the same pharmacology may produce one word in the record, because that may be the only word the observer has. Different patient populations and reasons for prescribing also contribute.

Canine "lethargy" may not be one symptom. It may be a compression artifact — an entire subjective symptom space flattened into the one thing a human can see from across the room.

This generalizes past pharmacovigilance. Every supervised model of an animal internal state — pain, anxiety, distress, welfare — trains on labels that are a human's inference about an animal that cannot be asked. There is no held-out ground truth, because the patient cannot be interviewed. That is the wall this work exists to attack.

Three bridges into pet psychiatry

Each connects an existing section of this site to a companion-animal phenotype with a public data source. Ordered by how directly the site already covers the mechanism. Not yet sourced: treat these as hypotheses, as explained under Provenance below.

Bridge 01 · Critical periods

The socialization window

The Evidence page argues critical periods are actively shut by molecular brakes, and uses songbirds to show the closing schedule is tuned by species. Dogs have the same structure with lifelong behavioural consequences: a puppy socialization window that closes in the first months, after which fear responses generalize and rarely fully reverse.

Bridge 02 · Compulsion

Canine compulsive disorder

Tail-chasing and acral lick dermatitis are treated as a naturally occurring model of human OCD — same drug class responds, and breed concentration implies genetic architecture rather than pure environment. A spontaneous animal model in a shared household environment, which no rodent model gets.

Extends SSRI · TrkB state on the Synapse Bench

Bridge 03 · Decline

Cognitive dysfunction

Canine cognitive dysfunction syndrome involves amyloid pathology and presents as disorientation, altered social interaction, and sleep-cycle disruption. Dogs live shorter lives and share our indoor exposures, so a longitudinal cohort covers adulthood to old age in years rather than decades. The dog-to-human age relationship is not a fixed ratio: a DNA methylation study in Labrador Retrievers found it is non-linear (Wang et al., Cell Systems 2020).

01

VeDDRA ↔ MedDRA crosswalk

No public mapping exists between the veterinary and human adverse-event vocabularies. Lexical normalization plus 17 hand-written synonyms currently matches 13 of the top 40 dog terms. Ontology alignment; embeddings are suited to it; the vet-only-drug negative control already exists in code.

Wedge
02

Non-verbal ground truth

Inferring internal states in a patient that cannot self-report. Underlies pain scales, welfare scoring, and every behavioural label in pet psychiatry. The hardest of these and the one with the widest reach.

03

Anthelmintic resistance as a reporting signal

69k lack-of-efficacy reports for heartworm larvae, 63k for flea preventives. Testable claim: failure reports cluster geographically and trend in time. Needs a reporting-effort control analogous to the German Shepherd comparison.

04

Bayesian shrinkage for small cells

EBGM/MGPS in place of raw PRR. Prerequisite for anything sliced by region × year × breed, where cells fall to double digits. Retroactively strengthens the MDR1 result.

Breed entity resolution

Owner-typed free text in an inverted taxonomy. 112k mixed-breed reports are currently invisible to any breed-stratified analysis.

Dose- and weight-adjusted exposure

The database carries dose numerator, unit, and animal weight. Breaks the count-only query model, so it needs new machinery before it needs new statistics.

Veterinary clinical NLP

Vet records are unstructured with no consistently applied coding standard. Far behind human clinical NLP. Largest gap on this list; also the one most dependent on data access.
